The Reality of Our Routine

Think about your day so far. You woke up, sat at the breakfast table, sat in your car or on the train, arrived at the office—and you sat down.

The staggering reality: The average office worker spends approximately 10 to 12 hours a day sitting down.

That is 70-80 hours a week, and over 3,500 hours a year. Over a 40-year career, we are spending nearly 16 solid years of our lives parked in a chair. We obsess over our beds, yet rarely question the quality of our office chair. Our bodies were designed to move—not to be folded into a 90-degree angle staring at a screen. We are paying a massive, silent price.

The Physical Impact

What sitting does to your body

📉

Muscles Shut Off

Electrical activity in your leg muscles shuts off almost immediately. Calorie burning drops to roughly one per minute.

💥

Spinal Pressure

Sitting puts up to 40% to 90% more pressure on your spinal discs than standing, leading to degeneration and chronic lumbar pain.

🤕

Cervical Strain

Slouching forward just an inch exerts up to 30 pounds of pressure on your cervical spine. Shoulders roll forward; hip flexors shorten.

"We normalize back pain as an occupational hazard. It is not a hazard of the occupation—it is a hazard of the equipment."

The High Cost of a Bad Chair

A poorly designed chair is an active contributor to bodily injury. A standard, rigid office chair forces your spine out of its natural 'S' shape and into an unnatural 'C' shape.

Without proper lumbar support, lower back muscles work overtime to keep you upright. Eventually, muscles fatigue, you slump, and spinal ligaments bear the load. Lack of adjustable armrests and seat depth cuts off circulation at the back of your thighs.

For companies, this translates to millions of dollars lost in productivity, increased absenteeism, and skyrocketing healthcare costs. We are crippling our workforce by seating them on cheap, poorly engineered furniture.
